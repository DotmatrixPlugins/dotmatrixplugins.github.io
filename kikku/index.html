Two kicks, one morph, no samples.

Kikku is a kick drum synthesizer. Kikku is Japanese for kick. Every kick is made from scratch: load one kick into slot A and another into slot B, and morph between them on the pad. Turn on XY SPLIT to take the low end from one point and the top from another, joined at a split you choose.

Eight pages shape every kick, from the pitch sweep and the click to three layers of drive, a noise layer, a filter, EQ that can act on just one moment of the hit, and a final clip. Start from 43 kicks and 25 kits, deal a new pair with RANDOM, and drag the finished kick straight onto a track.

What it does

  • Two kick slots, A and B, with a morph that blends the settings themselves, so the kicks never cancel each other out
  • XY SPLIT: the lows from one morph position, the highs from another, joined zero-phase at 40 to 400 Hz
  • AUTO keeps every point of the morph from getting louder than the two kicks it comes from
  • PITCH: tune, sweep start, time, curve, a slow drop across the tail, and a body from sine to rounded square
  • AMP: attack, hold, decay and decay curve
  • ATTACK: a click blended from TIGHT, SNAP, THUMP and WOOD on a field you drag, with its length, start phase and a transient softener
  • DRIVE: up to three layers from twelve styles, each with its own envelope
  • GRIT: hiss, sand or vinyl crackle that can outlast the kick
  • FILTER, three EQ bands that can act on a window of the kick, and CLIP in soft, hard, tape or tube
  • 43 factory kicks in ten styles and 25 kits; RANDOM, VARY, UNDO and REDO
  • LOOP on the beat at your project's tempo, KEY FOLLOW, velocity, and DRAG WAV (48 kHz / 24-bit)
  • Rendered ahead of time and played from memory: no latency, almost no CPU; dark or light panel, resizable from 60 to 200 %

AU, VST3 and CLAP instrument and a standalone app, for Apple Silicon and Intel Macs (macOS 10.14 or later).
